About Almond Ember Kitchen

Slow food, bold flavors, and the recipes worth repeating. This is a journal of deeply tested dishes, technique guides, and kitchen wisdom — written for cooks who care.

📅 Established 2019 📍 Portland, Oregon ✍️ Maya Chen, Editor

Our story

Almond Ember Kitchen began as a private notebook — a place to record the small discoveries that make cooking feel magical. The way a pinch of sugar transforms a tomato sauce. The exact moment butter turns nutty and brown. The ratio of salt to flour that makes bread sing.

In 2019, that notebook became this site. Today, it is a library of recipes that have been tested at least three times in a home kitchen, with every step, mistake, and breakthrough documented. We believe that good cooking is not about perfection — it is about paying attention.

Whether you are a seasoned home cook or just learning to hold a knife, we hope you find something here that makes you want to tie on an apron and stay a while.

“The best recipes are the ones you make again and again, until they become part of your muscle memory — and your family's story.”

— Maya Chen, Founder & Editor

What we believe

Test, test, test

Every recipe is cooked multiple times before it's published. No exceptions.

Flavor first

We chase depth, balance, and boldness — not just good looks on a plate.

Technique matters

Knowing why makes you a better cook. We explain the how and the why.

Slow is a virtue

Some dishes take time. That's not a flaw — it's the point.

Meet the kitchen

Maya Chen is a recipe developer, former line cook, and the voice behind Almond Ember Kitchen. She has spent over a decade in professional and home kitchens, and her work has appeared in several food publications. She lives in Portland with her husband, two children, and a sourdough starter named Doughlores.

When she's not testing recipes, she can be found at the farmers market, reading old cookbooks, or perfecting her pizza dough.

How we test

  1. First pass: We cook the recipe as written, following it exactly, taking notes on timing, texture, and flavor.
  2. Second pass: We adjust — more salt, less sugar, a different pan, a longer rest — and test again.
  3. Third pass: We ask a friend or family member to cook it from the written recipe without help. If they succeed, it's ready.

Only then does a recipe earn its place on the site. It's a slow process, but we believe it's the only way to build trust with our readers.

Behind the name

The “almond” is for the humble, versatile nut that appears in both sweet and savory cooking. The “ember” is for the fire — the heat, the transformation, the glow of a well-tended kitchen. Together, they represent what we do: coax flavor out of simple ingredients with patience and a little bit of heat.
